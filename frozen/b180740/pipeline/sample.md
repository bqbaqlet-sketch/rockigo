## Purpose of the well logs

A well log records a measured response along a borehole. A geologist compares responses recorded at the same depth before interpreting the type of rock.

<!-- page 2 -->
## Depth as a shared coordinate

Depth is the common coordinate for comparing measurements from different tools. A depth shift between tools can make two curves appear to disagree about a boundary.

<!-- page 3 -->
## Natural gamma radiation

A gamma-ray tool detects the natural radiation emitted by the surrounding rock. Some shale has a high gamma response, and some other rock can produce a high response too.

<!-- page 4 -->
## Comparing intervals

The shape of a log can help distinguish intervals. Start with a familiar reference interval, then compare nearby readings before proposing an interpretation.

<!-- page 5 -->
## A resistivity reading

Resistivity describes opposition to electric current. The measured response can depend on the rock, pore fluids and conditions near the borehole.

<!-- page 6 -->
## Pore water and dissolved salt

Dissolved salt affects how easily pore water conducts electric current. Two formations with similar rock structure may differ in resistivity because their water differs.

<!-- page 7 -->
## Pore space in rock

Porosity is the fraction of rock volume occupied by pores. The size and connectivity of those pores influence how easily fluid can move.

<!-- page 8 -->
## Connected and isolated pores

A laboratory sample may contain isolated voids that contribute to its total porosity. Such voids do not form a continuous path across the rock for fluid flow.

<!-- page 9 -->
## Changes near the borehole

When the borehole pressure exceeds formation pressure, some drilling-fluid filtrate can enter permeable rock. The effect depends on pressure, rock properties and the drilling fluid.

<!-- page 10 -->
## The scale of the measurement

Different tools respond to different volumes around the borehole. A reading near a thin boundary can be influenced by the neighboring layers.

<!-- page 11 -->
## Evidence for a decision

A proposed formation boundary should be consistent with the depth-aligned curves and with other observations when available. Record which evidence supports the interpretation.

<!-- page 12 -->
## Alternative explanations

A strong change in one curve can have more than one cause. Check whether a change in borehole conditions could explain the response before assigning it to the rock.

<!-- page 13 -->
## Recording the limits

An interpretation is useful when its assumptions are available to the next reader. Record the depth interval, the curves used, and the reason for excluding competing explanations.

<!-- page 14 -->
## Reviewing the interval

Before sharing a result, check depth alignment, units and the reference intervals. Save the observed response separately from the geologist's interpretation.
